Each word drops through the six layers. In each layer the router sends it to one of the eight experts, which lights up (its number counts the story's words it has had so far). After layer 6, out comes the next word, and that word drops in next. Click any word in the story to replay its trip.

2 · meet the experts of layer 3 — the colors in the story are these experts

These are not topic experts, like an animal expert or a space expert. Each one gets its own list of words, and in the higher layers those lists become grammar jobs: naming words, linking words, the ends of sentences.

what does “switched off” mean? (and a second kind of switch)

Switched off means muted: the router still sends the word to that expert, but the expert says nothing, so the word gets no help in that layer. Both stories always pick the mind's most likely next word, so the only difference between them is the switch.

: the router may not choose that expert at all, so each of its words goes to the expert it liked next best. That other expert can often do some of the work, so the story changes less.

    about this mind

    It is the same size and shape as the Big Storyteller (six layers), trained on the same children's stories, but each layer's thinking part is split into eight small experts, and a router picks one for every word. It writes as well as an ordinary mind of its size.

    Where do the names come from? A bigger A I (gpt-6.1-sol) read hundreds of words each expert gets and named it. Then another A I, given only that name, had to pick out which words go to that expert. A name is shown only if it got them right at least 75 times in 100. The others say “no clear job found”.

    Today's big AIs are often built this way, with experts and routers. This one also came in a version that sends each word to two experts: two experts per word write a little better but are harder to break.

    A mind made of experts: 19 million connections, of which 12 million work on each word. It learned from about two million children's stories.
