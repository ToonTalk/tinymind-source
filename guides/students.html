Student guide

Your field guide to Tiny Mind

Tiny Mind lets you raise, watch and look inside real language models. They are built the same basic way as the AI chatbots you have heard of, only thousands of times smaller, so you can see every part and switch parts off. Everything runs on your own computer.

How to be a mind scientist

Nobody can see "meaning" inside a model just by looking. Scientists make a guess, test it, and keep track of what happened. You will do the same.

1

Guess

Make a claim you could be wrong about: "This neuron is about food." "This part of the mind knows where Zog lives."

2

Predict

Before you press anything, write what should happen if your guess is right. "If I switch it off, the mind will stop saying moon."

3

Test

Switch the part off, turn it up, or swap it, and watch what changes. Change one thing at a time.

4

Decide

Was your guess busted or proved? Both are useful. A busted guess tells you something true about the mind.

A highlight is a clue. Switching it off is the proof.

Getting around

The front door

Tiny Mind opens on "What would you like to do?" with seven cards, one per room. Click a card to go in. ← all activities brings you back.

At the top of each room, the mind: menu shows which mind you are using and lets you pick another. Bigger minds download the first time you choose them; after that your browser keeps them.

Saving your work

💾 save my work puts the minds you raised and your storybook into one .zip file on your computer. To carry on later, or on another computer, choose 📂 open saved work, or a mind file… from the mind menu, or drop the file onto the page.

The guide

Every room has a guide in a panel on the right. Press ▶ for a spoken tour. Tick kid for simpler words, or voice to hear the guide read aloud. You can type a question in the box at the bottom.

Answering your questions needs an AI. The 🔑 button lets someone add a key for Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini. Ask an adult first: keys cost money and belong to whoever made them. The tour and every room work without one.

The seven rooms

The rooms are in the same order as the cards on the front door. You can visit them in any order, but raising your own mind first makes the Hunt more fun.

🐣

Teach a tiny mind

the Nursery

Invent a character, write it a storybook, and watch a tiny mind learn about it, step by step.

Try this

  1. In your character, give it a name, say what it is, and give it two special things. The example is Zog, a small dragon who eats clouds and lives behind the moon.
  2. Fill the storybook with 60 stories: 50 for the mind to learn from and 10 kept secret to test it. Write your own and press check + add, let the ghostwriter AI write them with ✍️ write 8 more stories, or load a ready-made storybook.
  3. Look at the model's whole alphabet. This mind knows only 512 pieces of words, so stories must use words it can spell.
  4. Press 🐣 start raising. Press ask for a story now whenever you like.

Notice

  • The pink line is the secret stories; the blue line is ordinary stories. Lower means less surprised.
  • When does your character's special thing start showing up in its stories?
  • Keep going too long and the pink line rises again: the mind starts copying its storybook instead of learning.

Challenge

Find the step where your mind knows its facts but still tells ordinary stories well. Save that mind with 💾 save my tiny mind (.bin) and take it to the Hunt.

No key needed, unless you want the ghostwriter AI to write stories for you.

⏪

Watch a mind grow up

the time machine

Replay recordings of minds learning, from random babble to whole stories. No waiting, and no mind needed.

Try this

  1. Pick a recording: a brand-new mind growing up, a mind learning your character (and then just memorizing), or a mind learning which facts belong to which character.
  2. Use ← Previous and Next →, or jump to a Moment.
  3. Give it a story start and press 📖 tell this story to hear the mind at that moment.

Notice

  • Open Questions about this moment. Try "Why does step 0 tell stories about Lily?" and "What is a held-out story?"
  • The tiles show whether the mind uses the name to pick the fact, and whether it mixes up characters.

Challenge

Find the first moment the brand-new mind writes a real sentence. Then find the moment the four-character mind stops mixing up whose facts are whose.

🔬

Look inside a mind

the Microscope

See which words a mind is weighing up, layer by layer, and find out what single neurons care about.

Try this

  1. Choose a mind. The Giant is best but downloads 440 MB once; the Big one is 58 MB; the Tiny one is ready at once.
  2. Type the start of a story and press 🔬 look inside.
  3. The guess-o-meter shows what the mind expects next. Layer by layer, and thinking ahead shows how that guess forms.
  4. Use the neuron finder, then open a neuron's card: what does this neuron do? Try 🔆 turn it up, 🔇 mute it and 🔄 turn it the other way.
  5. In the switch-off lab, check some heads and press re-run with checked heads OFF.

Notice

  • Each neuron has two ends: things that switch it on, and things that push it the other way.
  • Turning a neuron up almost always changes the story. Muting one often doesn't: other neurons cover for it.

Challenge

Pick a neuron and predict, in writing, what 🔆 turn it up will do to the story before you press it. Then find one attention head that changes the guess-o-meter's top word when it's switched off.

💭

See what a mind is thinking

the Thought bubble

Watch the words a mind is holding in mind as it reads or writes, then swap one of its thoughts and see the story change.

Try this

  1. Use the Giant or the Big Storyteller. Pick a story start and press 🔬 read it or ✍️ let it write on.
  2. In what it's holding in mind, choose thinking ahead to see words the mind expects later in the story, not only next.
  3. In swap what it's thinking, take one word out, put another in, and press 🔁 swap and rewrite. Compare the three versions.

Notice

  • Small minds oversteer: keep the strength low.
  • A famous swap: in a story about digging a hole, swap hole for castle and Max digs a moat.

Challenge

Find a swap that changes how the story ends but not how it starts. Then find a swap that does nothing at all, and explain why.

👥

Meet a mind made of experts

the Experts room

In this mind, a router sends every word to one of eight experts in each of six layers. Watch who gets which words, then switch an expert off and see what breaks.

Try this

  1. The Expert Storyteller downloads 77 MB the first time. Pick a beginning and press ✍️ write on. Each word is colored by the expert that handled it.
  2. Press ▶ watch the words travel through all six layers. Click any square to see that expert's card.
  3. On an expert's card, use 👁 watch to make its words glow, or 🔌 switch off to see the story without it.

The room's challenges

Switch off just one expert at a time, so you know which one did it.

  • Make the mind get stuck saying "to to to."
  • Make what the characters say go blank.
  • Make the mind write "said said."
  • Make the mind forget how to finish "let's" and "don't."
  • Make the mind say "wanted wanted."
  • In layer 2, "and" almost always goes to the same expert. Guess which one first! Then find an "and" that goes somewhere else.

These experts don't know about topics like animals or space. They sort words: one handles "to," another handles speech marks. Finding out what each one does is the puzzle.

👁

A mind that sees your drawings

the Tiny Eye

Draw something and a tiny mind guesses what it is. It knows 24 kinds of things, from clouds to castles. Then look at its neurons: most have one main job you can name.

Try this

  1. Draw with the mouse or a finger. Press 🎲 a doodle for a real drawing from Quick, Draw!, the game whose players made its pictures.
  2. Click a neuron that lights up to see its job, and 🔌 switch it off.
  3. In watch it grow, go back to see the eye early in its training, when its neurons had different jobs.
  4. In squeeze it, try an eye with only 8 neurons in its last stage.

Notice

  • It is never 100% sure, even of a perfect drawing. Open why is it never 100% sure?
  • Squeezed neurons each have to cover more kinds of things.

Challenge

Switch off two neurons so the eye can no longer tell a cloud from a sheep. Or draw something that isn't a dragon but makes it say dragon.

🎯

Hunt for a hidden fact

the Hunt

Find where a mind keeps something it knows, and prove it by switching parts of it off.

Try this

  1. The Hunt uses your latest raised mind if you have one, or a ready-made one about Zog or four characters.
  2. Pick a quest, such as "where does Zog live?", and press 🔎 step 1: ask the model.
  3. Press 🔬 is this really about this character? to check the mind answers differently for other names.
  4. Press 🕵️ get clues. The built-in detective works without a key.
  5. Test clues with 🧪 switch it off, or a whole team with 🧪 switch off ALL heads in layer.

Notice

  • When this hunt was first tested on Zog, switching off any single head or neuron left "moon" at about the same chance: 52–56%, from 55.6%. Switching off all of layer 0's heads dropped it to 22.8%.
  • Facts are usually shared by a team of parts, not kept in one spot.

Challenge

Prove a clue for your own character. Then show it matters for that character's fact and not for every story.

Words to know

Mind
What Tiny Mind calls a language model: a program that has learned to guess the next piece of text.
Piece (token)
The bits a mind reads and writes. Common words are one piece; a name like "Lily" may be two.
Training
Showing a mind lots of text and nudging its numbers so its guesses get better.
Secret (held-out) stories
Stories kept out of training, used to test whether the mind learned or just memorized.
Layer
One stage of the mind's work. Each piece passes through every layer, one after another.
Attention head
A part that decides which earlier pieces to look back at.
Neuron
One number inside a layer that goes up or down depending on what the mind is reading.
Switch off (ablation)
Silencing a part to see what changes. This is how you test a guess.
Chance (probability)
How likely the mind thinks each next piece is. 55.6% means about 56 times in 100.
Thinking ahead
Words a mind seems to be preparing before it writes them, found with a tool called the J-lens.
Router and expert
In the Experts mind, the router picks which of eight experts handles each word.

Things that surprise people

One part rarely knows a fact

Knowledge is spread across many parts. That is why single clues get busted so often in the Hunt.

Bigger isn't cleaner

Eyes 40 times bigger than the Tiny Eye guessed better, but their neurons were no easier to name.

Experts sort words

The Experts mind's experts mostly handle kinds of words, like "to" or speech marks, not topics.

Best score isn't best learning

A mind's stories can look their best before it has learned which facts go with which character.

Never 100% sure

The Tiny Eye was trained to keep a little doubt, so it shares a bit of chance among every other kind of thing.

Lily is everywhere

The stories these minds learned from mention "Lily" more than once per story, so brand-new minds love her.

If something goes wrong

It's very slow
Choose a smaller mind from the mind menu, and close other tabs. The Giant is the slowest to read a story.
A room says it's resting
Another room is using the memory. Go back to the room you used last and leave it, or reload the page.
The page looks old
Reload it fully: Ctrl+Shift+R (Windows), or Cmd+Shift+R (Mac).
The guide won't answer
Answering questions needs an AI key. The tour still works, and so does everything else.
The microphone doesn't work
Type your question instead. Inside the claude.ai version the microphone is always off; on the Tiny Mind website it works in browsers that understand speech, such as Chrome and Edge.
Training stopped
Keep the Tiny Mind tab visible while a mind is training.
